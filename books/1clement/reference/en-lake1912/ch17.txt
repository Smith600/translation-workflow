[17.1] Ler us also be imitators of those who went about “ in the skins of goats and sheep,” heralding the coming of Christ; we mean Elijah and Elisha, and moreover Ezekiel, the prophets, and in addition to them the famous men of old.
[17.2] Great fame was given to Abraham, and he was called the Friend of God,. and he, fixing his gaze in humility on the Glory of God, says “ But I am dust and ashes.”
[17.3] Moreover it is also written thus concerning Job :—* Now Job was righteous and blameless, true, a worshipper of God, and kept himself from all evil.”
[17.4] But he accuses himself, saying, “ No man is clean from defilement, not even if his life be but for a single day.’
[17.5] Moses was called “ Faithful with all his house,’ and through his ministry God judged Egypt with their scourges and torments; but he, though he was given great glory, did not use great words, but, when an oracle was given to him from the bush, ᾿ said :—* Who am 1 that thou sendest me? Nay, I am a man of feeble speech, and a slow tongue.”
[17.6] And again he says, “ But I am as smoke from a pot.”
